## Task and fixed calendar

Each prediction has a `(client, target_month)` key and a monthly total in mm. The nominal forecast origin is the preceding calendar month, and issue time is 00:00 UTC on the first day of the target month. Twelve historical origin rows form each neural input sequence. Bidirectional recurrence operates entirely inside that historical window.

| Role | Target months per client | Months | Client-months |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Gradient training | 1983-01 to 2007-11 | 299 | 1,495 |
| Inner monitor | 2008-01 to 2010-11 | 35 | 175 |
| Outer validation | 2011-01 to 2016-11 | 71 | 355 |
| Retrospective evaluation | 2017-01 to 2024-12 | 96 | 480 |

December 2007, December 2010 and December 2016 targets are embargoed from fitting, monitoring and selection. Under the declared CHIRPS release assumption, those December labels are unavailable at the following January 1 issue. They may enter later lagged covariates once the assumed release time has passed. All main methods share the same 480 evaluation keys; missing predictions cause a failure.

These are forecasts simulated using revised present-day archives and conservative calendar lags, rather than reconstructed historical data vintages. The monthly source support and assumed latency are saved. ERSSTv6, used for the reconstructed AMV predictor, postdates the evaluation period. Results must therefore be described as retrospective.

## Sources and feature groups

The target is the frozen CHIRPS v2 monthly final panel. The study domains use the frozen Natural Earth 5.1.1 India country geometry and recorded half-open client boxes. Country inclusion uses the native cell center; selected whole cells receive WGS84 geodesic area weights. Grids are retained at each product's native resolution. The domains do not cover all India, and Client 1 and Client 5 overlap; they are not spatially independent or an exclusive partition.

| Feature group | Predictor channels per historical row | Definition |
|---|---:|---|
| CORE | 14 | Four known target-calendar harmonics; four eligible rainfall lags; mean and population SD over three shifted rainfall windows |
| CLIMATE | 21 | CORE plus six corrected indices and ENSO × IOD |
| FULL | 43 | CLIMATE plus 22 meteorological channels: ERA5 7, ERA5-Land 11, NASA POWER 4 |
| TRAIN_SELECTED | 14, 21 or 43 | One existing group selected using only chronological gradient-training Ridge folds; an explicit alias |

The six indices are Niño3.4 SST anomaly, HadISST1.1 DMI anomaly, standardized CPC SOI, three-month ONI anomaly keyed by period end, bimonthly MEIv2 keyed by period end, and a distinctly named ERSSTv6 global-mean-removed AMV reconstruction. Absolute Niño3.4 SST is not the anomaly predictor. The old incomplete Kaplan AMO series is not spliced, extrapolated or renamed as the new AMV. Correlated Pacific indices are physical proxies and potential redundant predictors, not six independent causal mechanisms.

For a sequence-row origin `s`, main rainfall and index support ends at `s-1`, and meteorological support ends at `s-3`. Thus the latest target-relative support is target minus two months and target minus four months, respectively. The raw ENSO/IOD anomaly drivers used for climate severity remain unstandardized. All other transformations, local target mean/SD and the client heavy-rain threshold are fitted from gradient training only.

Native source units and canonical feature units are distinct. Native ERA5/Land temperatures are K, pressures Pa and monthly flow totals m/month. The portable assembler converts temperature to °C, pressure to hPa and monthly totals to mm/month; wind stays m/s and soil moisture m³/m³. POWER T2M is °C, RH2M percent, WS10M m/s and PRECTOTCORR monthly mean mm/day. POWER precipitation remains a rate feature. Source masks, area/count/coverage and temporal counts accompany regional values. Optional GLDAS preceding-three-hour physical semantics follow NASA/Google documentation; GEE custom start/end-hour properties have not been proved to be physical averaging bounds.

The accepted ERA5 archive contains 522 Earth Engine months plus six months recovered from pinned native archive snapshot `ZFKDHBCTBVHVXM3BQFV0`: 2022-04, 2022-05, 2022-07, 2022-08, 2024-01 and 2024-02. Earth Engine precipitation lacked 21 hourly validity values across these months, causing the strict joint mask to reject all five clients. All seven native variables and their complete support metadata are replaced for those six months, producing 30 recovered client-month rows. This is source reconstruction, with no statistical imputation or monthly-value clipping. The original failed Earth Engine export, recovery contract, native masks, provider controls and composition hashes are preserved. March 2024 remains the original Earth Engine compatibility control. January 1981 and March 2024 controls support the declared regional compatibility scope; they do not establish authenticated CDS bitwise equivalence or historical real-time vintages.

Solar data starting in 1984, GLDAS2.1 starting in 2000 and IMD are excluded from the main FULL schema. Solar/GLDAS may be evaluated only on separately named complete matched cohorts, with their no-extra-source controls refitted on exactly those keys. IMD includes 44 annual files covering 1981–2024, not a single-month source. Only Client 1 October 2023 falls below the 95% native-area coverage rule. A lagged IMD sensitivity must exclude all target windows affected by that invalid support, jointly across all clients, and refit its no-IMD comparator on the same cohort. No structural gap, invalid cell or missing index is filled. CHIRPS and IMD are both rainfall products; an IMD sensitivity is not evidence of source independence.

## Models and identities

The common neural backbone is `alce_joint_tanh_attention_v2`: two bidirectional LSTM layers, 48 hidden units per direction, a 16-unit climate embedding, nonlinear joint attention, regression head and optional heavy-rain head. Every neural method uses the same backbone and paired initialization within a feature group. The revised score is `vᵀ tanh(W[h_t, embedding] + b)`. This avoids cancellation of a time-constant climate term inside the historical linear scorer's softmax. A synthetic held-hidden intervention verifies that climate changes attention; disabling the embedding removes that pathway.

Implemented neural method IDs are `central`, `local`, `fedavg`, `fedprox`, `ditto`, `climate_graph` and `caaa`. FedAvg and FedProx exchange and aggregate parameter tensors. Ditto has separately counted global and persistent personal training streams. ClimateGraph-PFL is a declared study implementation with original, enriched or uniform training-only graph references; it is not labelled FedAMP or FedALA.

The primary CAAA comparison uses its **global** prediction identity, matched with global FedAvg and FedProx. Personal CAAA, post-update personal FedAvg/FedProx diagnostics, Ditto personal, local and graph-personal results receive separate names. FedAvg/FedProx personal diagnostics retain existing local updates; they do not add persistent personalized optimization. Each identity receives the same macro validation R² checkpoint rule. No global/personal result is silently substituted.

CAAA implements the additive sample-share, inner-monitor-performance and climate-relevance equation. The climate-relevance descriptor uses full-TRAIN mean absolute latest-row ENSO/IOD drivers and local TRAIN correlations; that descriptor is fixed across rounds. Inner-monitor performance can vary with the fitted round. Its separate bounded adaptive proximal coefficient uses eligible per-example ENSO/IOD states, clips each example's coefficient, then averages within the minibatch. Graph references and a validation router are distinct mechanisms; the CAAA equation contains no graph term. Any router containing pooled classical experts must be called a hybrid router and must disclose pooling. Router controls must use exactly the same fitted expert pool.

Five paired seeds are fixed: `42, 123, 2024, 7, 999`. Neural runs use all 60 rounds, three local epochs and batch size 32, without early stopping. At 299 training examples per client this means 9,000 optimizer steps and 269,100 examples per ordinary five-client run; Ditto's second stream is additional and counted. Central pooling is example-matched and has a different minibatch count. At most two CPU worker processes run, each with two CPU threads. Adam optimizer state resets each round. Actual optimization, example and byte logs are authoritative.

The predeclared development search is sequential: select a shared LR from {0.001,0.003} by FULL global FedAvg at seed 42; select μ from {0.001,0.01,0.1} for six named proximal methods; select CAAA coefficients from 15 quarter-simplex triples plus the (0.3,0.3,0.4) anchor. This is 36 symbolic candidates and 35 unique development fits, with exact tie rules, full curves and frozen choices before evaluation. Completed development selected LR=0.001, μ=0.1 for all six proximal methods, and CAAA coefficients (1,0,0); see [the development evidence](docs/DEVELOPMENT_CHOICES.md). With equal client training counts, the selected primary has uniform aggregation weights. Its performance and climate-relevance aggregation terms are inactive, although their configured metadata computations and adaptive proximal pathway remain disclosed. Feature/latency comparisons reuse those choices. All five FULL component controls instead use a separately labelled fixed (0.3,0.3,0.4) anchor parent, with the same selected LR/μ. This pre-score policy keeps every component active and avoids undefined removal of the only active coefficient. It estimates component effects at the anchor, rather than direct removal effects in the selected primary. Outer validation is development data because it selects both candidates and checkpoints.

TRAIN_SELECTED compares CORE/CLIMATE/FULL with pooled standardized latest-row Ridge α=10 on three chronological folds inside gradient training. It requires at least 0.002 mean macro-R² gain over CORE and otherwise retains CORE. Classical Ridge/ExtraTrees/RandomForest controls use latest-row raw pooled/local targets, no client-ID input, Ridge α=10 and trees with 256 estimators/minimum leaf 3/max_features=1.0. FULL flat-sequence ExtraTrees is a separately named representation sensitivity. FULL client-normalized pooled ExtraTrees uses training-only client mean/SD and inverse decoding to match the neural central target-loss/decode population; it remains distinct from raw pooled trees.

The corrected symbolic schedule contains 155 final neural fits, including five anchor parents, 135 classical fits, ten router bundles and one baseline bundle, in addition to the 36 development candidates. Each router bundle adds 18 chronological OOF expert fits. Identical TRAIN_SELECTED aliases save 15 neural and 30 classical fits; matching seed-42 development candidates may also be reused. Record the nine group-selector fits and all candidate/OOF work when comparing cost. Aliases are duplicate predictions, not independent evidence.

CORE and FULL each define a six-expert raw pooled/local Ridge/ET/RF router pool. Five controls share exactly the same fitted experts: best expert, equal weights, convex blend, revised robust temporal blend and training-OOF Ridge stack. Provisional weights calibrate on 2011-01–2013-11, score on 2014-01–2016-11, then refit static per-client weights through 2016-11. The revised robust objective is 0.65 overall plus 0.35 chronological last-half R². It is not the manuscript's tail-R²/penalty DPRC equation and is labelled a static temporal stability router. It has no climate gate, graph expert or neural expert.

## Evaluation and interpretation

Primary accuracy metrics are equal-client macro R² and macro RMSE in mm; only R² selects candidates/checkpoints. Pooled metrics are separately named diagnostics. Residual R² and the stated NSE formula are numerically identical, not independent evidence. Signed PBIAS uses 100×sum(observed−predicted)/sum(observed), so positive means underprediction; absolute bias is separate. Raw inverse-transformed predictions in mm are used for checkpoint selection and evaluation; negative predictions are retained and penalized.

Report all five seed scores, their mean and SD, per-client results, selected rounds, support counts, extreme-event diagnostics, optimizer/example counts, wall time and payload bytes. Seeds are repeats of training randomness, not additional independent rainfall observations. Statistical comparisons pair exactly the same seeds and target keys, and resample temporal blocks jointly across all clients and compared methods. The overlap between Clients 1 and 5 must remain represented. Validation rows never enter retrospective evaluation or its significance analysis.

Uncertainty uses 2,000 common-date circular block bootstrap replicates, seed 731, with primary block length 12 months and 6/24-month sensitivities. Dates and resampled five-seed indices are paired across methods and all clients. The estimand is mean per-seed macro skill, not a five-seed prediction ensemble. Saved deltas use A minus B: positive R² and negative RMSE favor A. The 31 comparison pairs are predeclared; 95% intervals are marginal, not simultaneous familywise bands.

Supplementary two-sided annual-block sign tests use eight blocks and 256 exact sign assignments. Their smallest possible nonzero p is 0.0078125, so Holm correction across 31 pairs cannot reject at 0.05. Report this resolution limitation; it is not a license to choose a more favorable test after evaluation. The main `revision_v2` inherited statistical policy enables `--pvalues`, frozen before study fitting.

The event threshold is each client's gradient-train 80th rainfall percentile, with strict observed>threshold labels. Trained neural event heads use probability≥0.5 with no post-hoc calibration. Methods without a trained head, including NO_AUX, use forecast rainfall>training threshold and are labelled continuous rainfall scores. Save per-client confusion counts, recall, precision/F1, AP, ROC AUC, PR curves and tail RMSE/MAE. An aggregate micro table must explicitly sum saved counts. Removing BCE differs from deleting a router expert. Monthly regional-average performance does not establish flood-warning capability.

## Privacy and cost boundary

Each fit simulates client/server operations within one CPU process; at most two fits run concurrently. Secure aggregation and differential privacy are not implemented. Model updates, counts, scaler sums/squared sums, target mean/SD and climate summaries are shared in cleartext. CAAA sensitivities need only client-local training correlations. The enriched rainfall-anomaly-correlation graph needs privileged offline access to aligned training rainfall anomalies; its setup applies to the graph family. Centralized comparators pool training data explicitly. These disclosures accompany results and diagrams.

Communication logs count tensor elements × dtype bytes and declared scalar payloads. Graph preprocessing, global scaling and training traffic are separate phases. Do not count the same graph artifact twice. The ledger excludes network headers, serialization and encryption overhead; measured CPU wall time is not network latency. Central/local network entries are not simulated and must be marked not applicable, with their data-access assumptions shown separately.

## Reviewer scope and release limits

The scientific protocol maps source, leakage, feature-redundancy, component, baseline, cost, extreme-event and statistical questions to concrete checks. Both frozen model phases and independent QA are complete. The selected primary has no general superiority over FedAvg/FedProx; global/personal outputs and separate classical routers must remain distinct. Related-work/novelty wording, the distinction from prior adapFL terminology and manuscript figure/text corrections require a separate author review. This repository alone cannot resolve them.

The literature correction is narrow: [MetePFL](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0393.pdf) already uses graph-based aggregation and adjacency regularization; [FedPoD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14244) combines a geographic graph with dynamic prompt-derived graphs for personalized collaboration. A claim that prior weather federated learning lacks graphs or personalization is unsupported. Any contribution here must concern the specified regional monthly rainfall task, predictor availability and measured component effects; the custom ClimateGraph-PFL implementation is not a reproduction of either method.
