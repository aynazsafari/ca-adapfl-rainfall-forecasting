# Original-paper comparison for the revision

Verified 8 October 2026. This bounded comparison addresses reviewer R2-05 (related-study matrix) and R2-06 (adapFL differentiation), with privacy, computation and tuning context for R1-02/03/07/08. All twenty exact reviewer comments and the manuscript audit were read. Ten original papers were selected: six algorithmic precedents and four precipitation/weather studies. This is a scoped comparison, not an exhaustive systematic review. No evaluation results were interpreted and no new baseline was added.

The manuscript's statement at P0037 that MetePFL lacks a similarity graph is contradicted by its Algorithm 1: learned prompt relationships feed graph generation, GAT and GCN aggregation. FedPoD also combines geographic and dynamic prompt graphs. Therefore graph use, personalization, adaptive aggregation, or geographic heterogeneity alone cannot establish novelty. The adapFL name is an existing name for FedAvg followed by client-specific fine-tuning; a common training budget does not make its final parameters uniform.

“Not reported” below refers to inspected methods and full-text searches, not a proof of absence. Numeric performance claims from other papers are deliberately omitted because targets, data, splits and units differ.

## Safe description of the revised scope

The revised CAA code performs real shared-parameter neural training. Its final server weights normalize a mixture of sample fraction, exp(−monitor loss), and absolute ENSO/IOD strength multiplied by fit-only client sensitivities. Its bounded proximal strength decreases with origin-available climate severity. Global and retained personal predictions are separate identities. The enriched graph is a separate personalized comparator: a fixed, fit-only mixture of aligned rainfall-anomaly covariance, seasonal climatology, multi-index sensitivities, and rainfall statistics. It does not enter the displayed CAA server-weight rule. These are implementation descriptions, not established gains.

The revision's climate-conditioned aggregation, explicit source-availability calendar, five Indian regional monthly CHIRPS targets, and declared physical descriptor construction distinguish its setting and mechanism from the cited prompt/radar/classification studies. They support a narrow contribution statement about the defined combination and its tested setting. This ten-paper comparison cannot prove that no earlier paper combines all components. Explainability must identify the actual fitted predictor being explained; it cannot make a central-control router into federated training.

The revised scaler communicates training summaries. Graph preparation additionally requires a privileged training-data covariance step and disclosed summaries. Thus raw training-record locality during model optimization is a limited design property; the revision is not a cryptographic privacy demonstration or a certified DP mechanism. Neither FedAvg/FedProx nor an “FL” label removes this disclosure obligation.

The 60-round, shared-learning-rate, sequential proximal/CAA coefficient sweep is a predeclared benchmark adaptation. Ditto's original per-client lambda tuning and the source papers' architectures/budgets are different. Describe the benchmark as matched controlled implementations, not exact reproduction of every original experiment. Physical graph variants and the central expert routers must retain their separate labels.
